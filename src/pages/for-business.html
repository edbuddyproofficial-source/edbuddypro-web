Why most AI training fails

Three ways it goes wrong

Every one of these is fixable, and each one shaped how the tracks are built.

Generic AI training doesn't survive Monday

A course that teaches prompting in the abstract leaves your team to work out how it applies to a variance report or a hiring rubric. Most never make the jump.

Tool tours age out in a quarter

Training built around one vendor's interface is obsolete the moment the interface changes. What lasts is the judgement about when to use AI and when not to.

No one can tell whether it worked

Seat licences get bought, completion is never measured, and the following year nobody can point at what changed in the actual work.

Fair questions

What sponsors usually push back on

All four are reasonable. Here is the honest answer to each.

“We already pay for ChatGPT licences

Then you have solved access, which is the easy half. The licence does not tell a recruiter which parts of screening are safe to hand over, or a finance analyst which numbers the model will confidently invent. That judgement is what the tracks carry.

“Our people can learn this from YouTube

Some will. The ones who do tend to already be comfortable with the tools. The value of a structured track is the middle of the distribution: the people who will not spend their evenings piecing it together, and who quietly keep doing the task the old way.

“We ran AI training last year and nothing changed

Usually because it was generic and nobody measured it. The two fixes are in this page: content written per role, and a pilot with agreed success criteria before a full rollout is bought.

“Our data cannot leave the building

Labs run in the learner's own tool accounts against material they control, so working documents do not pass through us. If your policy blocks public AI tools entirely, tell us at scoping; that changes which labs are usable.

Questions

Before you talk to us

How many seats do we need to start?

Tell us the roles and rough headcount and we will quote for that rollout. Volume pricing applies as the seat count grows.

Can we map the tracks to our own roles?

The five tracks are built around role workflows rather than job titles, so a people-ops analyst and an HR business partner both fit the HR track. At scoping we go through your org and agree which track each group takes.

Do our people need any technical background?

No. Every track is built on the assumption that nobody writes code at any point. The tools used are ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and the document and spreadsheet software your team already has.

What happens to the work they produce?

Each track carries ten projects built on real artefacts: a screening rubric, a variance commentary, a campaign brief. Those stay with the learner and with you.

How is our data handled?

Learners work in their own accounts on the AI tools, so their material does not pass through us. Course accounts and progress data are covered by our privacy policy and the DPDP Act.

What does reporting look like for an admin?

We agree the reporting with you at scoping. As a minimum you get enrolment and completion progress for every learner on the rollout.
