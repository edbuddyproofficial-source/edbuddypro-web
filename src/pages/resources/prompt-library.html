Copy-ready prompts for real work

30 prompts written the way we teach: a role, a clear task, context, a format and limits. Copy one, replace the [brackets] with your details, and paste it into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

HR

Inclusive job description

Draft a JD that has passed a bias check before it goes live.

You are a senior talent acquisition partner. Draft a job description for a [role] at a [company size, industry] in [city / remote].
They will [main responsibilities, 2–3 lines].
Use inclusive, plain language. Avoid gender-coded words, age-coded words and jargon such as "rockstar" or "ninja".
Format: a two-line summary, "What you'll do" (6 bullets), "What you'll bring" (must-haves only, 5 bullets), "Nice to have" (3 bullets). Under 350 words.
After the JD, list any phrases you changed or avoided and why.
HR

CV screening rubric

Agree the scoring criteria before you open a single CV.

You are an experienced recruiter. Build a CV screening rubric for a [role].
Must-have requirements: [list]. Nice-to-haves: [list].
Create 5–7 criteria that relate only to the work. For each, give a 1–4 scale with a one-line description of what each score looks like.
Exclude anything that could proxy for age, gender, religion, caste or background (for example graduation year, college name or gaps without context).
Format as a table.

Structured interview questions

Competency-based questions with what a strong answer sounds like.

Create a structured interview for a [role] assessing these competencies: [e.g. stakeholder management, problem solving, ownership].
For each competency, write 2 behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time…"), 2 follow-up probes, and a short description of a strong, an average and a weak answer.
Keep the questions job-related and legally safe to ask in India.

30/60/90 onboarding plan

An onboarding plan built for the role, not a template with the title swapped.

You are an HR business partner. Draft a 30/60/90-day onboarding plan for a new [role] joining the [team] team.
Context: [team size, manager, key tools, first big project].
For each phase give: goals, key meetings, learning, and one measurable outcome.
Format as three short sections with bullets, then a one-paragraph welcome note from the manager.

Balanced performance review

Specific, bias-checked review language at any rating level.

Help me write a performance review for an employee in a [role]. Rating: [e.g. meets expectations].
What went well: [2–3 examples with outcomes].
What needs to improve: [1–2 examples].
Write it using the SBI model (situation, behaviour, impact). Be specific and evidence-based; avoid personality labels such as "abrasive" or "not a team player".
End with two development goals for next quarter.

Variance commentary

Explain budget-vs-actual in words a business owner understands.

You are a finance business partner. Here is budget vs actual for [period] for [department]:
[paste table: line item, budget, actual]

Audience research questions

Questions for customer interviews that avoid leading answers.

I'm interviewing [type of customer] to understand [topic, e.g. why they switch tools].
Write 12 open, non-leading interview questions grouped into: their context, the problem, how they solve it today, and what would make them switch.
Then list 3 common interviewing mistakes to avoid for this topic.

SEO content brief

A brief that helps a writer create a genuinely useful page.

Create a content brief for an article targeting the search "[keyword]" for [audience].
Include: the reader's real question, a suggested title, an H2/H3 outline, points that must be covered, questions to answer, and what would make this more useful than a typical article on the topic.
Don't invent search volumes or statistics.

Cold email that sounds human

Short outreach that earns a reply.

What we offer: [one line]. Why it might matter to them: [specific trigger, e.g. they're hiring 20 sales reps].

Self-check before sending

Make the AI review its own answer.

Review your previous answer as a sceptical expert would.
1. List every factual claim, number or rule you stated, and mark each as "confident", "unsure" or "should be verified".
2. Point out any assumptions you made about my situation.
3. Flag any wording that could read as biased, unfair or too absolute.
Don't rewrite the answer yet. Just give me the list.
