What never to paste into an AI tool

The fastest way to lose trust in AI at work is one careless paste. Here is what to keep out, and how to still get the help you need.

First, check your company's rules

Many organisations have an approved AI tool and a policy for what can go into it. Where one exists, it overrides anything in this guide. If none exists, ask, and treat the list below as the minimum.

Depending on the tool, your plan and its settings, what you type may be stored, reviewed by the provider or used to improve future models. Business and enterprise plans often give stronger protections than free personal accounts, but you should only rely on that when your company has confirmed it. Until then, assume anything you paste could be seen by someone outside your organisation.

Keep these out

  • Personal details of real people. Names linked to anything sensitive, phone numbers, home addresses, ID numbers such as Aadhaar or PAN, dates of birth.
  • Pay and money about individuals. Salaries, bonuses, bank details, loans and deductions.
  • Health and personal circumstances. Medical information, disabilities, leave reasons, family situations.
  • Performance and disciplinary records that identify a person: reviews, warnings, investigations, exit interview notes.
  • Customer data. Contact lists, account details, order histories, support tickets with names.
  • Confidential business information. Unannounced results, deal terms, pricing strategy, legal matters, source code, passwords and access keys.

In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP) sets rules for how organisations process personal data. Sharing an employee's or customer's personal data with an outside tool, without a proper basis, can be a compliance problem as well as a trust problem.

The two-minute redaction habit

You can still get help with most sensitive tasks. Strip the parts that identify people or the business before you paste:

  1. Swap names for roles. "Priya Sharma" becomes "Employee A"; "Acme Corp" becomes "Client 1".
  2. Round or band numbers. "₹14,85,000" becomes "about ₹15 lakh", or "mid-band for the role".
  3. Remove dates and locations that would make a person easy to identify.
  4. Describe instead of pasting. Instead of pasting a full investigation note, describe the pattern: "an employee has been late to client meetings four times this quarter".
  5. Put the details back yourself after the AI has done the drafting.
Don't pasteRahul Mehta (Emp ID 40213, salary ₹18.2L) has had two written warnings for missing deadlines on the Infosys account. Draft his PIP.
Paste this insteadA mid-level account manager has missed three client deadlines this quarter and has had two written warnings. Draft a 60-day performance improvement plan with clear, measurable goals, weekly check-ins and the support we will provide.

Tasks that are usually safe

Plenty of valuable work involves no sensitive data at all:

  • Drafting templates: job descriptions, policies, onboarding plans, email formats
  • Explaining concepts, regulations or frameworks in plain language
  • Brainstorming questions, agendas, training ideas and survey items
  • Rewriting your own text for tone, length or clarity
  • Working with data that is already public or fully anonymised

Some decisions should stay with people

Even with clean data, don't let a chatbot make decisions about people on its own: who to reject, who to promote, who to let go. AI can help you structure the decision, draft the communication and check your reasoning for bias. The decision, and the accountability for it, stays with a person.
